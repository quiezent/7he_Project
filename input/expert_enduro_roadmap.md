## Role Of This Roadmap

This is the durable, human-readable block plan for moving Clayton from experienced toward expert enduro performance. It fixes the calendar, intended adaptations, promotion gates, and review rhythm so weekly plans can be adjusted without losing the long-term direction.

It is not automatic same-day clearance and does not contain immutable daily prescriptions. The canonical strategy remains in `config/athlete_context.json` and `config/coaching_architecture.json`. Exact execution comes from fresh Garmin evidence, `snapshots/coach_packet.json`, `snapshots/weekly_plan.json`, CNS readiness, and dated schema-v3 session contracts. The lowest safe ceiling always wins.

From 2026-08-26 onward, this roadmap replaces the future macrocycle intent of `snapshots/enduro_reacquisition_plan_2026-08-10_2026-09-12.json`; that older artifact remains historical evidence and must not be deleted.

## Performance Destination

Clayton's expert-rider objective is upper-echelon, repeatable execution rather than isolated aggression:

- hold speed into and build speed out of difficult corners without reactive braking or rescue corrections;
- remain dynamically centred with front-tyre authority and deliberate bike-body separation in very technical terrain;
- scrub appropriate jumps with controlled trajectory, landing placement, and exit composure;
- tolerate unavoidable heavy or imperfect landings without rearward collapse, chassis instability, pain, or next-day cost;
- manage controlled rear movement and neutral two-wheel drift, and recover from incidental front micro-slip without panic input;
- preserve vision, decision speed, braking precision, line choice, and corner exits on the final stage of the day;
- absorb a controlled practice-style day and retain race-quality execution on the next permitted key day.

## Non-Negotiable Architecture

- Sunday remains Sabbath hard rest. The only current exception is the named PDR26 race on Sunday 2026-09-20; Monday 2026-09-21 is the exact replacement Sabbath and is hard no-exercise.
- Build toward 5-6 unique bike days when recovery and calendar allow, but keep only 2-3 sessions meaningfully costly.
- Protect two MTB exposures when possible: one Stumpjumper quality/fitness day and one Enduro durability or race-transfer day.
- A third MTB exposure is normally capped skill transfer. If it becomes costly, it replaces the structured engine session or another costly session.
- The Stumpjumper carries routine fitness and technical volume. The Enduro is normally limited to one training exposure per week; Denai recce consumes that exposure. Official practice and racing are event exposures.
- Use one structured engine session per week. Torque, VO2, threshold, and repeatability are not separate mandatory sessions in the same week.
- New skill, new trail, setup testing, maximum speed, and fatigue testing must not be stacked.
- A stop-rule trigger ends or downshifts the written dose. Continuing after a trigger cannot validate the nominal contract.
- Seven-day primary sleep should first stabilize at 6.5 hours or more before expanding costly work or using a 6-7-hour training week as anything more than a ceiling.

### PDR26 Success Evidence

Race success is not reduced to placing or Garmin load. Review:

- clean-run count and whether each run had an explicit purpose;
- line memory and braking-point repeatability;
- vision and decision speed from first practice run through race run;
- front tracking, dynamic centre, brake release, and exit commitment;
- arm pump, braking fatigue, upper-body fatigue, and any rescue or near miss;
- fixed equipment behaviour: pack-down, kick, pitching, wallow, skitter, bottom-out, or pedal strikes;
- carbohydrate, fluid, and sodium execution;
- explicit stop-rule outcome during practice;
- next-morning and 48-hour response.

## Promotion Gates

### Continuity And Absorption

- Average about five unique bike days across three consecutive weeks when calendar and recovery permit.
- Protect two MTB exposures and keep meaningful-cost sessions at three or fewer.
- Stabilize seven-day primary sleep at 6.5 hours or more before expanding costly work; progress toward 7 hours.
- Do not add duration after a stop-rule override, technical degradation, abnormal next-day response, or two-session accumulation that threatens the next protected MTB day.

### Controlled Engine

- Torque progression remains `3x8 -> 3x10 -> 3x12` before raising watts.
- Progress only when every repetition preserves cadence and form, local-leg RPE remains within the written ceiling, the stop rule is not triggered, and next-day response is clean.
- VO2 entry dose is normally `4x3 min` before `5x3`, with cadence 85-95 rpm and less than about 5 percent power falloff. Exact watts require current operational FTP, RPE/HR validation, and a trustworthy power-source comparison because the Suito may under-read above about 200 W.
- Use a standardized 75-90-minute endurance anchor under comparable cooling and fueling; approximately 5 percent or less aerobic decoupling is the promotion target.
- A maximal FTP test is optional validation after continuity is stable, not a prerequisite for training.

### Enduro Repeatability

Progress from three complete Twin Peaks cycles to four, then repeat the four-cycle proof on a separate day. Each proof requires:

- final climb RPE no greater than 6/10;
- final-run vision, decision speed, brake release, front tracking, dynamic centre, line accuracy, and exit quality within one subjective point of the first run;
- braking and upper-body fatigue no greater than 3/10;
- no reactive braking, rescue, near miss, or late skill fade;
- `stop_rule_outcome: not_triggered`;
- race-relevant fueling and a clean next-morning response.

Four short 2K/2K+ descents do not equal four complete Twin Peaks climb-stage cycles. Reported capacity for another run is reserve evidence, not an executed proof.

### Expert Skill Ceiling

- Cornering: at least five of six clean repeats, braking completed before turn-in, intended exit reached, no mid-turn rescue, and final two repetitions equal to the first two.
- Jump squash/scrub: matched entry-speed window, reduced trajectory without worse landing placement or exit composure, at least five of six clean landings on two separate days, and video from useful angles.
- Landing robustness: stable chassis and active ankle, knee, hip, and elbow absorption with no rearward collapse, bottom-out, rim strike, rear kick, pain, or next-day joint response. Do not manufacture repeated flat landings.
- Sliding: progress from controlled rear yaw to neutral two-wheel drift, then recovery from incidental front micro-slip. Use predictable low-consequence ground and never deliberately provoke a high-speed front washout.
- Very technical terrain: sight at 60-70 percent, confirm line and braking markers, then progress to 80-85 percent. Add only one of speed, roughness, fatigue, or novelty at a time.

## Fueling And Recovery Standards

- For hot MTB sessions of 90-150 minutes: 45-75 g carbohydrate, 500-900 ml fluid, and 600-1000 mg sodium per hour, selected from same-day heat, sweat response, consequence, and gut tolerance.
- For sessions over 150 minutes or race practice: 60-90 g carbohydrate, 650-1000 ml fluid, and 800-1200 mg sodium per hour.
- Fuel skill quality early. Late braking errors, weak pumping, timid jumps, or poor line choice can be heat or carbohydrate failures rather than a pure skill ceiling.
- No intentional pre-PDR weight-loss phase. Post-race body-composition work is optional and must preserve power, sleep, recovery, and technical quality.
- US-market work cutoff and sleep opportunity are part of the training dose. Additional intensity is not the remedy for an unabsorbed week.

## Digital-Twin Use

The digital twin is an experimental comparator, not Clayton's ceiling or an automatic governor. Improvement means moving the dose-response frontier: more work at the same RPE and recovery cost, the same work with less drift, a deliberately progressed contract completed cleanly, or equal final-run quality after greater accumulated work.

Use a standardized anchor every two to four weeks. Progress one lever at a time. Habitually exceeding the written dose or continuing after a stop trigger creates off-policy evidence; it may still inform delivered-action, execution-boundary, and safety-adherence lanes, but it does not validate the nominal prescription.
